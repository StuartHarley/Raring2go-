## 0. The terms, in plain English

- **ARR (annual recurring revenue):** what your subscriptions are worth over a year. Take what customers pay you every month for the software, and multiply by 12. One-off fees don't count. Example: 39 centres paying £399 a month is £15,561 a month, or **£186,732 ARR**. If they paid only £49 a month it would be **£22,932**. Your 39 centres are across versions, so the real answer sits somewhere in that range. Investors use ARR to judge what a company is worth.
- **Loaded cost:** what a hire really costs you, not just the salary: employer National Insurance, pension, equipment and software.
- **Pre-money / post-money:** a company's value before and after the investment. If Fujifilm puts in £1m for 10%, the post-money value is £10m and the pre-money is £9m.

## 4. Equity: a sliding scale inside your 0–30% range

Principle: **0% for the licence and channel paths (B and C)**; for investment (A), the stake grows with the size of the round, with Fujifilm's total holding capped at **30%**.

| Level | Suggested stake | Reasoning |
|---|---|---|
| 1 · Foundation | **5–10%** (midpoint 7.5%) | Small, early round |
| 2 · Accelerate | **10–20%** (midpoint 15%) | Typical strategic minority (benchmarks say 10–20%) |
| 3 · Scale | **20–30%** (midpoint 25%) | Larger commitment, at the top of your range |

**Implied company value at the midpoint stake** (post-money = raise ÷ stake):

| | Level 1 (7.5%) | Level 2 (15%) | Level 3 (25%) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1 · Print core** | £6.7m | £7.0m | £10.6m |
| **2 · Print + publishing** | £9.3m | £9.7m | £14.4m |
| **3 · Whole suite** | £10.7m | £11.7m | £17.2m |

Context: UK seed pre-money valuations are around £4–6m (British Business Bank and European data differ); a corporate investor normally takes 10–20%; early-stage SaaS is often priced at roughly 4–6× ARR (up to 8–15× if growing fast). **The headline cell (Scope 2, Level 2) implies about £9.7m post, or £8.2m pre, which at 6–10× ARR needs roughly £0.8–1.4m of ARR.** If your real ARR is lower, expect the valuation to rest on traction and the Fujifilm relationship, or accept a larger stake for the same money. Please check this before the meeting.

**Terms to avoid giving:** a right of first refusal on any sale of the company, and long-term exclusivity. Both are commonly requested by strategic investors and both limit your exit price.

## 5. Option terms (page 25)

**B · Licence.** Fujifilm would offer your software to its own customers under its own brand and pay you for each centre. Suggested opening position: Fujifilm pays **about 70% of the guide price per centre** (about £279 of £399 a month; negotiate 65–75%), and **guarantees at least 100 centres in year one**, which is about **£335,000 a year** to you. Fujifilm keeps the difference between what it charges and what it pays you.

**C · Channel.** Fujifilm sells your software alongside its presses and earns a commission. Benchmarks: reseller margin 15–30%; referral fees 10–25% of the first year's value. Suggested: **20% of each subscription** for as long as Fujifilm services the customer (about £80 of £399 a month), or a **15% one-off fee on the first year** (about £718 per centre).

**What I'm asking you:** are those good opening positions, or do you have your own figures? They only set where negotiation starts.
